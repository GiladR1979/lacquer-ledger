Check for duplicate nail polish before buying

A practical pre-shopping routine for exact duplicate shades, similar colors, and wishlist checks using your nail polish inventory.

Two bottles can look nearly identical in a shop and very different on the nail. A collection record can help you avoid buying the exact same bottle twice, but it cannot decide whether two different formulas are interchangeable. Separate an exact duplicate check from a personal comparison of similar shades.

Check the exact brand-and-shade pair

Search for the brand or shade before buying. Compare the printed name with your saved record, including any number or collection name that distinguishes a release. A familiar color alone is not enough: brands sometimes use related names for different products.

Lacquer Ledger warns when a record with the same brand and shade already exists. The comparison ignores capitalization and surrounding spaces. “Example Polish” and “example polish” therefore do not become different brands merely because you typed them differently. This is an exact name check; it does not analyze photographs, compare color formulas, or recognize every alternate spelling.

Handle look-alikes with notes

If the new shade has a different name, inspect your inventory for similar choices. Check the finish and your notes as well as the color family. A pink cream, a pink shimmer, and a sheer pink topper may serve different purposes even when a small bottle photo looks similar.

Write the reason you would choose one over the other. “Sheer topper over dark colors” is more useful than “pretty pink.” If you cannot remember the difference, add the candidate to your wishlist and compare later instead of treating a database search as a guarantee.

Use a short shopping routine

  1. Search the exact brand and shade on your shelf.
  2. Check your wishlist for an existing reminder of the same bottle.
  3. Read notes on the closest shades you own.
  4. Decide what this purchase adds: a new finish, a replacement, a gift, or simply a color you want.
  5. After buying, keep the ownership status and notes current.

These steps also work with a simple spreadsheet. An offline app is convenient when you want the record available without signing in or depending on reception. Lacquer Ledger stores its active collection on your device and does not sync it to another device.

When you intentionally own two

A spare bottle or a gift can be intentional. A duplicate warning is a prompt to check, not a claim that the purchase is wrong. Use a clear note about why the second bottle exists. Do not rename an existing shade just to hide the warning; that makes the next search less reliable.

Make a small test with a fictional record first: save “Example Polish / Rose Window,” then try adding the same pair with different capitalization. The warning should appear. Try a different shade name and notice the limit of the check: a new name is not a visual color match.
