How to organize a nail polish collection

Build a useful nail polish inventory with brand and shade names, photos, wishlist entries, and a quick pre-shopping check.

A nail polish inventory is most useful when it answers a question at the moment you need it: “Do I own this shade?” Start with the bottles you reach for most often, rather than waiting for a perfect afternoon to catalogue everything. Ten accurate records are more useful than a long list with unclear names.

Start with brand and shade

Put the manufacturer in the brand field and the printed shade name in the shade field. Keep those two details separate. For example, a fictional bottle might be brand “Example Polish” and shade “Rose Window.” Use the same brand spelling for every bottle from that maker. If the label is hard to read, make a note instead of guessing the name.

In Lacquer Ledger, open Add, enter the brand and shade, choose your shelf or wishlist, and tap Save polish. Finish, color family, notes, and a bottle photo are optional. You can start with the required details and add context when it becomes useful.

Make photos help you recognize a bottle

A clear picture of the front label makes a search result easier to recognize. If you also want to remember how a polish looked on you, describe the lighting, coats, and top coat in notes. A bottle photo and an on-nail result answer different questions; do not rely on a phone screen as an exact color match.

Choosing a photo copies that selected image into the app's private storage. Lacquer Ledger does not upload the image. Keep your original photographs separately if they matter to you: a CSV export contains bottle details and wear fields, but it does not contain photos.

Separate owned bottles from future purchases

A wishlist is a place to remember a possible purchase, not proof that the bottle is on your shelf. Choose the appropriate status when adding it. Before shopping, filter the shelf and search for the brand or shade. Then check your wishlist separately to avoid confusing a planned purchase with something you already own.

Use notes for details that the printed name cannot capture: “gift from May,” “looks warmer in indoor light,” or “compare with the rose shimmer at home.” Consistent, concrete notes make later searches more useful.

Try a five-bottle setup

  1. Add three bottles you own with their exact brand and shade names.
  2. Add two shades you are considering to the wishlist.
  3. Search for one brand, then switch between shelf and wishlist.
  4. Check that each record's status matches reality.
  5. Export a CSV and open it to confirm you can recognize the records outside the app.

The goal is a collection you can keep current. Add new bottles when they arrive and record changes before details are forgotten. For repeated purchases, read the duplicate-check guide. To rediscover bottles you already own, try the wear-log routine.
