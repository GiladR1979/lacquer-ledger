Keep a nail polish wear log and rotate your collection

Use wear counts and recent choices to rediscover nail polish favorites, with a simple rotation routine and an honest guide to CSV exports.

A shelf list tells you what you own. A wear log adds a different clue: what you actually reach for. Tracking wears can help you notice a neglected favorite or a shade that suits your current routine. You do not need a challenge, a public feed, or a strict schedule to make the record useful.

Record a wear when you choose the bottle

In Lacquer Ledger, find the polish on your shelf and tap Wearing today. The Wear tab shows recent choices and wear counts. Record the event at a consistent moment, such as after finishing your manicure, so that you do not accidentally count the same wear twice.

A wear count is a count of recorded uses. It does not measure how much polish remains, how many days the manicure lasted, or how often someone else used the bottle. Use it as a personal memory aid. If you start logging today, older bottles may have low counts simply because their earlier wears were never recorded.

Choose a rotation that fits your week

Pick three possible shades from your shelf: one reliable favorite, one bottle you have not chosen recently, and one that suits an upcoming occasion. At your next manicure, choose whichever feels right and record that wear. The point is to make your options visible, not to turn the collection into an obligation.

After a few choices, look at recent wears. If you repeatedly choose the same finish, that can guide future shopping. If a bottle remains untouched, your notes can explain why: awkward application, a color preference that changed, or a shade reserved for a particular season. The app does not infer those reasons for you.

Keep useful observations with the bottle

Use concrete notes you will understand later. “Two coats over a ridge-filling base” or “prefer this in daylight” gives more context than a general rating. Lacquer Ledger's notes belong to the bottle record; they are not a separate diary entry for every manicure.

For a fictional example, imagine “Rose Window” has five recorded wears and “Night Orchard” has none. That does not mean Rose Window is objectively better. Night Orchard might be new, or you might simply have started the log after its last wear. Counts need context.

Make a portable copy

Tap Export above the collection to create a CSV through your device's share sheet. Save it somewhere you can find, and open it once to check the records. The export includes bottle details and wear fields. It does not include photos, and version 1.0 does not import CSV files, so a CSV is not a complete in-app restore backup.

The active collection stays on your device. Lacquer Ledger has no account or app-operated cloud sync. Before removing the app or moving devices, read the support page and your device's backup settings; keeping a CSV separately preserves a readable list even though it cannot recreate the app's full state.

Start with one recorded wear, then review the list after your next few manicures. To tidy the underlying shelf, use the inventory guide. Before adding more bottles, run the duplicate-check routine.
